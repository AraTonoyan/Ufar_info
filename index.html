About UFAR

The French University in Armenia (UFAR; French: Université Française en Arménie; Armenian: Հայաստանում Ֆրանսիական Համալսարան) is a French-language higher education institution established in 2000 through a collaboration between the Armenian and French governments. Located in Yerevan, Armenia, at 10 Davit Anhaght Street in the Kanaker-Zeytun district (the IMA branch is situated at 1 Paruyr Sevak Street, also in Kanaker-Zeytun), UFAR offers French-language instruction across various disciplines, blending French academic excellence with local cultural understanding.

According to Armenian law, UFAR operates as a foundation and enjoys the strong support of Armenian public authorities. The institution is officially recognized by the Ministry of Education, Science, Culture and Sport of the Republic of Armenia and is co-governed by representatives of the French Embassy, the French Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Armenian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the Armenian Ministry of Education.

UFAR is unique among French-diploma-awarding institutions outside France: unlike comparable institutions such as Cairo University (which has only a French pro-rector) or the Hanoi Francophone Institute of Information Science, UFAR's regulations require that both the Rector and the Secretary General must be French nationals. The current Rector is Salwa Nacouzi.

The university awards dual state diplomas recognized by both Armenia and France — a feature embedded from UFAR's founding pedagogic principle. Its programs are designed to meet the needs of the Armenian labor market and the regional economy of the Caucasus, while simultaneously equipping graduates for careers in France, Europe, and beyond. Since 2003, UFAR has partnered with the Armenian General Benevolent Union (UGAB) to send deserving students for 7-week internships in French companies and public institutions, with more than 200 students having benefited from this program over 15 years.

History & Timeline

UFAR's founding is rooted in a long history of Franco-Armenian diplomatic and cultural collaboration, formalized through a series of official agreements spanning the 1990s.

1991

Independence of Armenia

Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the Republic of Armenia establishes diplomatic relations with France, laying the groundwork for educational cooperation.

November 4, 1995

Cultural Cooperation Agreement

A landmark agreement on cultural, scientific, and technical collaboration is signed between Armenia and France, officially recognizing the desire of both governments to establish a joint higher education institution.

November 23, 1998

Founding Protocol

A subsequent protocol is concluded between the Armenian Ministry of Education and the Embassy of France in Armenia, specifying the operational and pedagogic framework for the future university. Jean Moulin Lyon 3 University is designated as the primary French academic partner.

2000

UFAR Established

The French University in Armenia officially opens its doors, offering Bachelor's degrees in Law, Management, Finance, and Marketing. From the outset, UFAR adopts the principle of awarding dual Armenian and French state diplomas. The university becomes the mainstay of Francophonie in Armenia.

2003

UGAB Internship Partnership Launched

The Armenian General Benevolent Union (UGAB France) joins UFAR to offer 30 selected students annually the opportunity to complete their mandatory 7-week internship in French companies and public institutions, broadening the Franco-Armenian professional network.

2014

TEMPUS & Erasmus+ Projects

UFAR coordinates a TEMPUS project on "Promoting quality and recognition of transnational education in Armenia and Georgia" and an Erasmus+ project on Cycle 3 Quality Assurance, positioning itself as a regional leader in higher education quality standards.

c. 2018

Faculty of Computer Science (IMA) Expansion

The Faculty of Computer Science and Applied Mathematics (known as IMA) is significantly expanded. A new dedicated building opens at 1 Paruyr Sevak Street, Kanaker-Zeytun, Yerevan. Bachelor's and Master's programs in Informatics and AI are developed in partnership with the University of Toulouse 3 Paul Sabatier.

2020–2022

PRME Accreditation — First in Armenia

UFAR becomes the first and only university in Armenia to receive international accreditation under the United Nations' Principles for Responsible Management Education (PRME) initiative, aligning with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

2023

Master's in AI with Toulouse 3 Paul Sabatier

A new Master's program in Artificial Intelligence — the only AI Master's in Armenia with military deferment rights — is offered in partnership with the University of Toulouse 3 Paul Sabatier, with instruction in English and French.

2024

WBL4JOB Conference & Work-Based Learning

UFAR hosts the concluding WBL4JOB conference (November 20–22) for the EU-funded project "Introducing work-based learning in higher education systems of Armenia and Moldova for better graduate employability," with participation from the University of Strasbourg, IMC University of Applied Sciences, and Lyon 3. The Apprenticeships Higher Education Model (AHEM) is showcased.

2025

Dual Accreditation & European Quality Forum

UFAR's double accreditation is highlighted at the European Quality Assurance Forum. PhD programs in Law, Marketing, and Management are listed as available postgraduate pathways. Applications for the 2025–2026 academic year open March 3.

Bachelor's Degree Programs

All Bachelor's programs at UFAR follow the Bologna Process framework: 8 semesters, 240 ECTS credits, with mandatory internships. Upon graduation, students receive two state diplomas — one from UFAR (Armenian) and one from a French partner university.

8 semesters 240 ECTS In-person Dual Diploma 7-week internship

At the end of the Bachelor's cycle, students complete a 7-week internship in a host organization (in Armenia or abroad), and submit a written internship report for evaluation.

Language of Instruction & French Pathway

No French Required at Admission

Proficiency in French is not required to enroll at UFAR. The university provides a structured French language pathway that prepares students fully within the first two years of study.

While French is the primary long-term language of instruction, UFAR's pedagogy is structured to accommodate students with no prior French knowledge. During the first two years, students study core subjects alongside intensive French language courses. Their acquired knowledge is assessed using an internationally recognized language test. The continuation of studies beyond year 2 is conditional on passing this test.

As a result, approximately 90% of students who had not studied French before entering UFAR become capable of proceeding with their studies in French from the third year onward.

Thanks to visiting professors from Jean Moulin Lyon 3 University and Toulouse III - Paul Sabatier University, at least 20% of undergraduate courses (from year 3) and approximately 50% of graduate courses are conducted in French. After completing their mandatory internship in France, Belgium, or Armenia, students submit and orally defend their graduation thesis in French.

UGAB France Partnership — Paris & Lyon Internships

Since 2003: 200+ Students Trained in France

Since 2003, the Armenian General Benevolent Union (UGAB France), with support from the Bullukian Foundation, has partnered with UFAR to offer 30 selected students annually the opportunity to complete their mandatory 7-week internship in a French company or public institution in Paris or Lyon. In 15 years, more than 200 students have benefited from this program.

The internships offer a unique experience in the French professional world, encouraging the success and future employment integration of Armenian students. They build personal and cultural ties between Armenia and France and introduce students to French entrepreneurial methods and professional culture.

🌐 UN PRME — Principles for Responsible Management Education

UFAR is the first and only university in Armenia to receive international accreditation under the United Nations' Principles for Responsible Management Education (PRME) initiative. PRME is a global movement to develop responsible leadership through management education aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). As UN Secretary-General António Guterres noted: the PRME initiative was launched to encourage future responsible leaders at a moment when bold and innovative leadership is essential for achieving the Sustainable Development Goals.

🇫🇷 French Diploma Recognition

UFAR's dual diploma agreements with Jean Moulin Lyon 3 University and Toulouse III – Paul Sabatier University ensure that UFAR diplomas are recognized by the French Ministry of Higher Education. This recognition allows graduates to work, continue studies, or pursue academic careers in France and across the European Union.

🇪🇺 Erasmus+ Programme

UFAR participates in Erasmus+ International Credit Mobility (ICM) for student and staff exchanges with European partner universities. Armenia falls under Region 2 (Neighbourhood East) in Erasmus+ geographic classification.

📚 TEMPUS Projects

UFAR coordinated the TEMPUS project "Promoting quality and recognition of transnational education in Armenia and Georgia," establishing a four-tier management approach and national management teams in both partner countries.

UFAR participated in the EU-funded project "Introducing work-based learning in higher education systems of Armenia and Moldova" culminating in the November 2024 conference. Partners included the University of Strasbourg and IMC University of Applied Sciences.

UFAR has been a member of the Agence Universitaire de la Francophonie (AUF) since 2011 — the world's leading network of French-language universities. Since 2011, UFAR hosts the Chaire Senghor de la Francophonie, part of the international Senghor Chair network. The chair promotes research, conferences, and French-language education on legal and cultural topics, with a focus on comparative law and Francophone issues in the South Caucasus — including conferences on surrogate motherhood, corporate governance, and intellectual property.

💻 SAP Partnership

UFAR has partnered with SAP, the global enterprise software company, to enhance digital skills training for students. This industry link provides students with practical exposure to leading enterprise technologies and strengthens employability in the IT and finance sectors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I choose UFAR?
UFAR offers high-quality French and Armenian bilingual education with internationally recognized dual degrees (both Armenian and French state diplomas), strong career prospects, mandatory internships in France or Armenia, and direct access to the Francophone professional world — all at tuition fees calibrated to the Armenian standard of living.
Is a UFAR diploma recognized internationally?
Yes. UFAR diplomas are co-awarded in partnership with Jean Moulin Lyon 3 University (Law, Management, Finance, Marketing) and the University of Toulouse 3 Paul Sabatier (Computer Science / AI Master's), making them officially recognized by the French Ministry of Higher Education and valid throughout the European Union and beyond.
Do I need to speak French to apply?
No. French proficiency is not required at the time of admission. UFAR provides intensive French language training during the first two years. Approximately 90% of students who had no prior French become capable of continuing their studies in French by the third year.
Does UFAR provide scholarships?
Yes. UFAR offers excellence scholarships for top-performing students and need-based financial aid for students with challenging social circumstances, thanks to private donors and partner organizations such as the Bullukian Foundation and UGAB.
Can I complete my internship in France?
Yes. All Bachelor's and Master's internships can be completed in Armenia, France, Belgium, or other countries. UGAB France provides 30 students annually with the opportunity to intern in French companies and public institutions.
Does UFAR have exchange programs?
Yes. UFAR participates in Erasmus+ International Credit Mobility and has bilateral agreements with partner universities in France and other countries, allowing students to study abroad and gain international experience.
What student clubs and organizations exist at UFAR?
UFAR has an active Student Council (Conseil des Étudiants) that coordinates sports clubs, cultural organizations, academic societies, and other student unions. Regular activities include sports events, cultural festivals, employer networking events, and guest lectures.
Does UFAR provide student housing?
UFAR does not have on-campus housing, but the university assists students in finding accommodation in Yerevan near both campuses (Davit Anhaght Street and Paruyr Sevak Street).
What are the official working hours for the administration?
Monday to Friday, 09:00–17:45. You can also reach UFAR by phone, email, or through the online student portal at bureau.ufar.am.
Is the AI Master's program only for UFAR graduates?
No. The Master's in Artificial Intelligence accepts both UFAR graduates and external applicants. It is the only AI Master's in Armenia that confers the right to military service deferment by government decision.
What makes UFAR unique compared to other universities in Armenia?
UFAR is the only institution in Armenia to offer Franco-Armenian dual state diplomas, the only university with UN PRME accreditation, the only institution where both the Rector and Secretary General are required to be French nationals, and one of very few Armenian universities providing full transnational education with extensive quality assurance experience.
What postgraduate options are available after a UFAR Bachelor's?
Graduates can continue at UFAR with a Master's degree (Law, Management, Finance, Marketing, or AI/Computer Science), or pursue further education at French partner universities (Lyon 3, Toulouse 3) or other institutions in Europe. PhD programs in Law, Marketing, and Management are also available at UFAR.

Per UFAR's founding regulations (codified by the 2017 Franco-Armenian bilateral decree), the Rector and Secretary General of UFAR must be French nationals appointed by the French government — a unique governance structure that distinguishes UFAR from all comparable Francophone institutions outside France. The Vice-Rector is Zaruhi Soghomonyan. The Head of International Relations is Gohar Ghalachayn.

UFAR operates under Armenian law for its Armenian state diplomas and under French law for its French diplomas — a dual legal framework that ensures full recognition in both countries. The Board of Directors includes equal numbers of Armenian and French appointees, with representatives from both governments and partner universities.
